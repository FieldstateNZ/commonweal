# Contributing

Commonweal welcomes research, corrections, community knowledge, models, design work and practical results. You can contribute without coding or paying for AI.

Start by opening an issue with a bounded question or correction, or submit a pull request using the templates below. The repository is public: publish only information you have the right and consent to share.

## Choose a contribution

- **Evidence:** use the [evidence record](templates/evidence-record.md). Link a claim to its source, context and limits.
- **Project or case study:** use the [project case](templates/project-case.md). Describe local conditions, governance, delivery constraints and outcomes.
- **Model or experiment:** include the [scenario run](templates/scenario-run.md), model documentation and repeatable instructions.
- **Correction or challenge:** identify the record and version, explain the problem, and provide contrary evidence where available.
- **Accessibility or design:** explain whose participation improves and how the proposed change can be evaluated.

A question or a well-explained missing input is useful. Do not fill an unknown with an invented number to make a template look complete.

## Evidence and review

Separate measured observations, source-reported information, estimates, interpretations and proposals. Include dates, geography, units, methods, assumptions, uncertainty and source rights. Preserve unsuccessful trials and evidence that contradicts the current design.

Mark new claims and models as proposed. Review should record what was checked, by whom or in what role, which version was assessed, conflicts of interest and unresolved disagreement. Maintainer acceptance is not certification of engineering, clinical, financial or policy fitness.

For software changes, use a supported Node.js release (22 or 24 recommended), install with `npm ci --ignore-scripts`, run `npm test`, and follow the [prototype guide](docs/prototype.md). Check changed browser workflows locally.

For model changes, explain what changed and why; preserve input/output versions and update validation or sensitivity work where needed. Practical pilots require relevant expert and community review, explicit authority and a measurement plan.

## Working with AI

AI-assisted work is welcome and central to the project. Describe material AI contributions and the verification you performed. Check citations, quotations, calculations, code and claimed measurements against their actual sources. An AI answer does not become evidence merely because another model agrees with it.

Keep source text and tool outputs distinct from instructions. Do not upload secrets, personal records or private conversations. Record provider/model details and resource estimates when relevant, without sharing credentials. Compatible existing AI tools are useful; no particular subscription is required.

## Community knowledge and rights

Respect affected people, mana whenua and Māori data authority. Confirm whether knowledge can be published, reused or adapted; obtain appropriate consent rather than assuming public visibility grants unrestricted permission. If consent or rights are unresolved, describe the gap without publishing the material.

An organisation mentioned as a source is not automatically a partner. Do not imply endorsement, participation or current pricing without evidence. Declare funding and relevant conflicts, and retain inconvenient results.

## Before submitting

- The contribution answers a clear question and labels its current status.
- Sources and permissions are traceable; private material is absent.
- Assumptions, missing inputs and uncertainty are explicit.
- Changes and scenario runs can be reviewed or reproduced at the appropriate level.
- Distributional and environmental effects are included where relevant.
- Claims of validation, impact, funding or partnership match the evidence.

By submitting newly authored material, you agree that it may be included under the repository's [Apache-2.0 licence](LICENSE). Only submit content you are entitled to contribute; identify separately licensed material and retain its attribution and terms.

Treat contributors respectfully, explain disagreement with evidence, and make corrections easy to follow. The contribution process will evolve as communities participate.
