# Proposed first paid research pilot

Status: prospective scope, 28 September 2026. No commissioner, community host, reviewer, funding or revenue is confirmed. No outreach or application has been made. The current Commonweal app is a local illustrative annual screening prototype; its operational usefulness, real-world model validity and paid demand remain unproven.

**Question:** can a documented community-energy approach help one other community make a better decision, and can the research be delivered at a sustainable cost?

The proposed engagement is six to eight elapsed weeks of research, adaptation and review for **one named New Zealand community**. It is not a construction project. Solar is the first study within Commonweal's wider mission of a comfortable life independent of paid employment, freedom to earn, create or enjoy leisure, and environmental recovery. This pilot cannot demonstrate that energy projects can provide all essentials or replace employment income.

## Scope and delivery

| Stage | Proposed work | Reviewable output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2: establish the question | Agree the local decision, responsible host, affected groups, data rights, reviewer and scope. Reconstruct supported Ōtaki facts from dated sources and list unresolved discrepancies. | Decision brief, evidence register, data inventory, source permissions and explicit gaps. |
| Weeks 3–4: adapt and compare | Replace relevant assumptions with agreed local information. Compare no new project with two bounded alternatives; test at least three agreed uncertainty cases. Keep ownership and benefit-delivery arrangements explicit. | Versioned inputs, reproducible JSON runs, documented formulas, comparisons and sensitivity notes. |
| Weeks 5–6: challenge the findings | Have a community practitioner and relevant energy specialist review the model boundary, inputs and interpretation. Record corrections, disagreements and unsupported conclusions. | Review record, corrected model/results where needed, remaining limits and a decision recommendation. |
| Weeks 7–8, if needed: hand over | Resolve agreed review actions and run a workshop in which the host changes an input and reproduces a comparison. | Public-safe report, reusable evidence/code, run instructions, cost/resource account and a recorded next decision. |

AI remains central to evidence research, coding, design exploration and review preparation. Sources and arithmetic receive independent checks; AI-generated claims do not become verified evidence. Record material AI contributions, tools/model versions where available, corrections and compute expenditure. Preserve unknown energy, water, embodied and ecosystem impacts rather than entering zero. Reuse local calculations and compatible existing AI access; the engagement does not require a new paid inference service.

The current engine separates annual energy balances, capital cost, operating value and hypothetical benefit shares. **Avoided bills are not necessarily cash available to distribute.** Actual project cash flow, financing, replacements or transfers require a separately specified and tested extension, agreed within the pilot scope, or remain explicit unknowns. A benefit-sharing proposal must identify who owns the assets, who pays each cost and how renters or other households could receive benefits. It must not count the same saving twice.

## Entry gates and acceptance

Work starts only when there is a willing local host with authority over a real decision; agreed participation and data arrangements, including relevant community and mana whenua interests; a contracting entity and payer; and named reviewer roles with time and fees agreed. A community's location should follow a real need, not a grant opportunity. Necessary confidential inputs can stay outside the public repository; publication needs suitable rights and consent.

A specialist must agree that the chosen calculation is suitable for the bounded question. If it is not, narrow the question, change the method within an agreed scope, or stop. A network connection, outage resilience, engineering or investment decision needs the appropriate separate assessment; this pilot supplies no approval to build.

Acceptance means:

- The source-based reference reconstruction and local comparisons can be repeated from a clean setup, with model/input versions and evidence links preserved. Missing observed outcomes remain missing.
- Every material input is sourced with units, period, method and limitations, or visibly marked as an assumption or unknown. Reported observations, forecasts and illustrative scenarios remain distinct.
- Energy balances reconcile. Financial boundaries separate costs, avoided bills, cash receipts and any proposed transfers; unresolved cash availability prevents claims of distributable money.
- The baseline, two alternatives and at least three sensitivity cases are documented. Environmental gaps and distributional trade-offs remain visible.
- Review findings and corrections are recorded, and the host can change one input and reproduce the comparison.
- The host records a decision: proceed to a specified specialist assessment, collect named missing information, revise the proposal, or stop. A well-supported negative finding fulfils the engagement.

No household savings guarantee, revenue guarantee, winter reliability, grid feasibility, net climate benefit or national scalability follows from these outputs. Enlarging a site, replicating elsewhere and changing the whole energy system require different evidence and models.

## Transparent compensation and cost estimate

The following is a **bottom-up planning estimate, not market pricing, a supplier quote or evidence of willingness to pay**. It covers prospective work after the scope is agreed, not recovery of past prototype costs.

| Work | Estimated hours |
| --- | ---: |
| Evidence and provenance | 35 |
| Reusable calculation work and tests | 45 |
| Local adaptation and sensitivity analysis | 35 |
| Documentation, review response and handover | 30 |
| Coordination | 15 |
| Base effort | **160** |
| Explicit contingency allowance | **24** |

Budget formula: **184 hours × agreed sustainable contributor rate + independent reviewer quote + capped direct expenses**. Publish the agreed categories, material conflicts, actual effort and lessons without exposing personal information. Pay substantial practitioner/reviewer work explicitly; community participation arrangements also need agreement.

For illustration only, an assumed NZ$100/hour gives NZ$18,400 for contributor time. A provisional NZ$2,000 reviewer allowance and NZ$600 direct-expense cap would give **NZ$21,000 before any applicable GST**. Neither the rate nor allowances are researched market prices. The expense cap could include a separately visible compute allowance of up to NZ$300; no spend is authorised by this document. Replace allowances with quotes and agreed participation costs before fixing a fee. If they do not fit, reduce scope or revise the proposal before work begins.

Proposed payment milestones are accepted scope/data inventory, working comparison, and reviewed handover. A commission pays for research, adaptation and stewardship; the public core and appropriately licensed reusable findings remain open. Record actual cost and value delivered to test whether future commissions can fairly fund contributors and operations.

## Prospective commissioning and referral routes

- **Electra: direct commissioning hypothesis.** Its public community programme supports Kāpiti/Horowhenua resilience and names Energise Ōtaki's Warm Up programme; it also provides distributed-generation connection guidance. That makes a comparison for a local community a plausible question to explore. No research budget, purchasing interest or relevant open grant is established. Electra's existing support is not a Commonweal partnership. [Community programme](https://electra.co.nz/electra-in-the-community/), [connection guidance](https://electra.co.nz/connect-solar-or-distributed-generation/).
- **Trust Tairāwhiti: conditional regional funding route.** Its published Feasibility Fund offers up to **NZ$25,000** for eligible feasibility work. A genuine Tairāwhiti project and regional benefit, an eligible legal-entity applicant, financial contribution and supplier proposal are required; routine operations and retrospective work are excluded. A local applicant would need to confirm that this prospective paid scope and publication terms qualify before starting funded work. The fund is not a commitment to support Commonweal, and this estimate does not establish eligibility. [Fund information](https://trusttairawhiti.nz/apply-for-funding/feasibility), [application guidelines](https://trusttairawhiti.nz/assets/Documents/Funding-Application-Guidelines/Feasibility-Fund-application-guidelines.pdf).
- **Community Energy Network: referral and practitioner route.** Its remit connects community organisations and energy-sector partners. It could help identify a member with a current decision and a budget owner. No grant programme for Commonweal was verified; its own requests for sponsorship are not an offer to fund this pilot. [CEN remit](https://www.communityenergy.org.nz/about-us/).

Energise Ōtaki is a source owner and possible factual reviewer, not an agreed partner or payer. Any independent review invitation needs a concrete scope, appropriate credit and agreed compensation. Existing research and third-party model assets retain their own reuse terms; a citation does not authorise copying them into this repository.

Before any future authorised outreach, prepare the demonstration and a one-page scope. Test whether a prospective commissioner has a current decision, accessible inputs, a budget owner and a purchasing route. Five qualified discussions without those conditions, adequate existing free/adviser support, inaccessible data, or delivery costs exceeding buyers' budgets would challenge the demand hypothesis. Polite interest, referrals, downloads and grant eligibility do not establish demand. No outreach, application or external commitment is authorised by this plan.
