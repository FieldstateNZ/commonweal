# First study: adapting community solar

Status: bounded public reconstruction and illustrative software prototype, 28 September 2026. The [evidence account](evidence.md) records public annual totals and gaps. The [annual model](../../docs/prototype.md) uses invented examples; no partner agreement, validated local adaptation or practical pilot is complete.

## Question

How can AI help a community assess and adapt a documented solar project, making its costs, delivery conditions, distribution of benefits and environmental effects understandable and testable?

This is one bounded study within the wider mission of unconditional comfortable living. Electricity generation, a community fund and universal household energy provision are different outcomes.

## Reference and evidence boundary

Energise Ōtaki reports that its Power Up Ōtaki solar installations supply the wastewater treatment plant and Ōtaki College, sell excess electricity to the grid, and direct proceeds to a community fund. That is a useful reference for reconstruction; it is not evidence that all households receive free electricity. [Project source](https://www.energiseotaki.nz/power-up-otaki), accessed 28 September 2026.

The source is an account by the project organisation, not an independently verified dataset. We have not established its full current costs, contracts, generation history or distribution of benefits. Publishing a reconstruction would not imply endorsement or partnership with Energise Ōtaki or any named organisation.

## Work and next steps

1. Create a case record and evidence register from appropriately licensed public sources.
2. Identify missing inputs and distinguish historical figures from current quotes.
3. Reconstruct flows of energy, money and responsibilities using the simplest appropriate model.
4. Compare a baseline, smaller design and larger design where inputs support doing so.
5. Use AI to propose and examine an adaptation for a second community, replacing local assumptions explicitly.
6. Seek relevant technical and community review before anyone relies on the model for a practical decision.
7. Publish results, errors and unresolved questions; plan a pilot only if authority, resources and review are in place.

The contribution would be an editable, sourced model and delivery record. Existing guidance should inform it: [Ara Ake's Community Energy How-to-Guide](https://www.araake.co.nz/project/community-energy-how-to-guide) covers governance, finance, connections and operation.

## Inputs to establish

| Area | Required information |
| --- | --- |
| Need and access | Demand over time, reliability and comfort needs, beneficiaries, renter access and exclusions |
| Resource and design | Local weather/generation, siting, system size, storage, losses and replacement |
| Network | Connection limits/costs, import/export arrangements, capacity and curtailment |
| Ownership and finance | Asset control, capital, funding terms, maintenance and allocation of proceeds |
| Delivery | People, skills, compensated work, materials, permissions and lead times |
| Environment | Lifecycle emissions, power, water, materials, waste, land and ecosystems |
| AI and computation | Research/design role, verification, compute budget and resource-accounting gaps |

Do not present unsupported costs, generation or returns as project evidence. Invented software demonstration inputs must remain explicitly illustrative and separate from reported data. Values must carry source dates, units, currency/price year and uncertainty.

## Local adaptation and wider effects

Separate enlarging one site, repeating the design in another place, and the consequences of many projects. Include changing export values, demand profiles, network congestion, storage needs, workforce/material constraints and distributional effects. A successful local case is not a national savings multiplier.

For later energy-system context, assess [EECA's TIMES-NZ scenarios, assumptions and data](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/insights/data-tools/new-zealand-energy-scenarios-times-nz/) for suitability and reuse rights. Existing national scenarios do not validate this proposed local reconstruction.

## First completion criterion

A reader can trace every consequential input, reproduce a bounded comparison, see who benefits and under what conditions, and identify what must be checked before action. A qualified reviewer can challenge the model. Missing evidence, contrary findings and failed designs remain visible.
